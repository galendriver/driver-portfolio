### **Appendix D: Glossary**

**ARM (Adjustable Rate Mortgage):** Mortgage with interest rate that changes periodically based on market conditions. Risk: Rate can increase; Benefit: Often starts lower than fixed rates.

**HELOC (Home Equity Line of Credit):** Revolving credit line secured by home equity. Can borrow up to limit, pay back, borrow again. Interest usually variable.

**RSU (Restricted Stock Unit):** Company equity compensation that vests over time. Taxed as ordinary income at vest (not at grant). After vest, behaves like regular stock.

**Pre-IPO equity:** Stock in private company before it goes public. Illiquid (can't sell) until IPO or tender offer. High risk/high reward.

**Tender offer:** Opportunity to sell private company stock before IPO. Company or investor buys shares at set price. Rare liquidity event.

**IPO lockup:** Period after IPO when insiders/employees can't sell stock. Typically 90-180 days. After lockup expires, selling pressure often hits stock.

**Target-date fund:** Mutual fund that automatically adjusts allocation based on retirement year. Gets more conservative as target date approaches. "Set and forget" option.

**Sector fund:** Mutual fund or ETF focused on specific industry (healthcare, financials, etc.). More concentrated than broad market, higher risk/reward.

**Tax-loss harvesting:** Selling investments at a loss to offset capital gains. Reduces tax bill. Can carry forward unused losses indefinitely.

**Basis (cost basis):** Original purchase price of investment. Used to calculate capital gains. Lower basis = higher taxable gain when sold.

**Long-term capital gains:** Profit on investments held >1 year. Taxed at favorable rates (0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income). Much better than ordinary income rates.

**SALT cap:** State and Local Tax deduction limit. Capped at $10,000 for federal taxes. Limits benefit of high property taxes and state income taxes.
